--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

VYTRA is an offline Android app for ASHA workers.

During a household visit she photographs two things with the phone she already
carries:

  1. The inner pink of the lower eyelid (lid gently pulled down)  →  anemia risk
  2. The outer white of the open eye                               →  jaundice risk

The phone, with no internet, returns:

  Low  /  Moderate  /  High  /  Unable to assess

plus a one-page referral PDF she can send on WhatsApp or print.

It does not print a hemoglobin or bilirubin number.
It does not say “you have anemia.”
It is a triage aid, not a diagnosis, not a medical device.
A PHC doctor still does the real test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

India can diagnose anemia and jaundice in a facility. The gap is earlier, at
the house.

  • No lab, no hemoglobinometer, often no 4G.
  • The ASHA has a phone, a register, and a few minutes.
  • “You look pale” does not travel to the PHC doctor.
  • NFHS-5: a majority of Indian women 15–49 are anemic.
  • Families often never walk in until it is late.

Every alternative fails a field constraint: blood tests need travel; gadgets
need money and training; cloud AI needs internet and uploads a face photo.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW A VISIT WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Language (Telugu / English)
       → Consent (refuse = camera stays off)
       → Skin-tone + lighting note
       → Photo of plain white paper (corrects room light)
       → 2–3 lid photos, then 2–3 eye-white photos
       → Risk class on one screen, with a full medical disclaimer
       → Optional name typed only for the PDF, then forgotten
       → Share PDF

If she cannot get two clean photos of one part, that part is
“Unable to assess” — never silently marked Low.

Works in airplane mode. Zero rupees per test. No extra hardware.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT “AI” ACTUALLY IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a cloud model. Not a hemoglobin predictor.

On the phone, the app converts the colour of the chosen pixels to CIELAB
(a standard colour space), looks at redness (a*) on the lid and yellowness
(b*) on the eye-white, and puts that into three prototype buckets.

Face Mesh is used only to aim at an open eye. When the lid is pulled, the
mesh often fails — a drawn ring still works. That is designed, not a bug.

Thresholds are prototype heuristics. They are not Tamir 2017 cutoffs
(that paper used RGB on 19 people). We cite it as prior art, not as our accuracy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT V1 WILL NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  • Newborn / neonatal jaundice (Face Mesh is an adult-face model)
  • Hemoglobin or bilirubin numbers
  • “Clinically validated / 95% / ICMR approved”
  • Extra lenses, Hindi-first UI, ABDM/ABHA, iOS, Play Store
  • Skin or teeth scanners as the main product (optional internet demos only;
    do not put them on the college PPT)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIVACY (ONE PARAGRAPH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The photograph is analysed in memory and discarded. The database stores a risk
class, time, phone model, skin-tone category and lighting — never a name, photo
or GPS. A name typed for the PDF is written on the PDF only, then dropped.
Records become eligible for deletion after 30 days.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE SENTENCE (PORTAL / SLIDE 1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

VYTRA is an offline-first Android screening aid that lets an ASHA worker
photograph the everted lower eyelid and the temporal sclera, receive anemia
and jaundice risk classes (low / moderate / high / unable to assess), and
share a referral PDF — with no internet, no consumables, and no claim of
medical diagnosis.
